## 2. Library-degree mismatch vs. PREREGISTRATION.md §2 (pilot: addressed; Koopman/EDMD: addressed; symbolic regression: now addressed — both degrees run/compared, method itself unreliable at either)
PREREGISTRATION.md §2 freezes the SINDy library at "polynomial terms up to degree 3." `experiments/pilot_chaos_vs_periodic.py` now runs **both degree=2 and degree=3** and reports both (RESULTS.md, `experiments/pilot_chaos_vs_periodic_results.json`; degree-2-only numbers preserved verbatim in `experiments/pilot_chaos_vs_periodic_results_degree2.json`). Finding: the non-chaotic-regime 0/5 result is degree-invariant, but the chaotic regime's "5/5 at every noise level" degree-2 headline does NOT hold at degree=3 — it drops to 3/5 (Lorenz, noise>=1%) and 2/5 (logistic, noise=5%), confirming this item's prediction that the degree-2 library mechanically inflated apparent recovery cleanliness.

**Koopman/EDMD: addressed this session.** `src/discovery_koopman.py`'s `MonomialDictionary`/`fit_edmd` were already degree-generic (the `degree` parameter was never hardcoded to 2 in the dictionary construction itself); `scripts/run_koopman_smoke_test.py` was extended to fit and report BOTH degree=2 (10 observables) and degree=3 (20 observables) on the same Lorenz trajectory/seed, comparing one-step prediction error, rollout error, and local spectral (eigenvalue-vector) error against the analytic Jacobian. Finding: on this clean, densely-sampled trajectory, all three accuracy metrics improved at degree=3 (one-step relative RMS error 0.0037%→0.0000%; rollout error at horizon=300 steps 0.0108→0.0000; spectral error 1.5613%→1.5551%), but the fitted K's condition number rose ~1000x (2.14e1→2.17e4) — the predicted overfitting/conditioning risk of a larger dictionary is real and visible even though accuracy did not degrade on this particular trajectory. Reported both ways per instructions, not selecting the more favorable result; see CLAIM_LEDGER.md/EVIDENCE_INDEX.md.

**Now addressed (this session):** `scripts/run_symbolic_regression_smoke_test.py` now runs `fit_symbolic_regression()` at **both** max_degree=2 and max_degree=3 on the identical Lorenz trajectory/derivatives/seed=0 and reports both side by side (structural match, R^2, is_polynomial, degree_ok, per dimension) — no longer defaulting to one undocumented degree. This closes the library-degree-mismatch gap for symbolic regression in the same sense it is closed for SINDy and Koopman/EDMD (a genuine both-degrees comparison exists and is reported), but running both degrees does **not** make the method reliable at either: see item 3 for the honest degree-2-vs-degree-3 result (both FAIL the smoke test).

## 3. Symbolic-regression degree restriction not enforced or verified (enforcement: fixed; discovery method itself unreliable at BOTH degree 2 and degree 3)
Previously `max_degree` was accepted but never checked, and the docstring referenced a nonexistent `simplify_program_degree`. Fixed: `src/discovery_symbolic_regression.py:45-115` now implements `program_polynomial_degree()`, a real tree-walk over gplearn's `._program.program` prefix-notation list that computes polynomial degree and flags non-polynomial terms (e.g. `x/y` with a variable denominator correctly reports `is_polynomial=False`; `x/2.0` with a constant denominator correctly reports degree 1). Hand-verified against 6 constructed programs, all matching expected values. `fit_symbolic_regression()` calls this every fit attempt inside a retry loop (`max_degree_retries`, different `random_state` per retry) that keeps the best degree<=max_degree polynomial attempt found, and returns per-dimension `degree_ok`/`is_polynomial`/`degrees`.

`scripts/run_symbolic_regression_smoke_test.py` now runs and gates on this at **both** max_degree=2 and max_degree=3 (same Lorenz trajectory, same derivatives, same seed=0), reporting a side-by-side comparison table rather than only the degree-3 run. **Result, reported honestly (not the more favorable outcome):** the search is unreliable at *both* degrees, not just degree 3, and for partly different reasons per degree:
- `x` and `z` dimensions: structural match True, R^2 >= 0.99997, `degree_ok=True` at both degree=2 and degree=3.
- `y` dimension is the persistent failure at both degrees. At max_degree=2, gplearn found a genuine polynomial (`is_polynomial=True`) but its true degree still exceeds 2 after 5 retries (`degree_ok=False`), and its fit quality is R^2=0.9547 (below the 0.99 gate). At max_degree=3, gplearn instead settled on a nested rational expression with a variable-containing denominator (`is_polynomial=False`, `degree_ok=False`), with R^2=0.9697 — still below the 0.99 gate. Neither degree cap fixes the y-dimension's fit-quality shortfall or produces a clean degree-respecting polynomial for it within the retry budget.
- Aggregate gates: `max_degree=2` → structural_match=True, degree_ok=False, fit_ok=False. `max_degree=3` → structural_match=True, degree_ok=False, fit_ok=False. **Both smoke-test runs FAIL**, honestly reported rather than picking the degree that happens to pass.

**Closed for this item's original scope** (enforcement/verification is genuinely correct and now exercised/reported at both preregistration-relevant degrees, not just asserted). **Still open, and worse than previously documented:** this is not a degree-3-specific weakness — gplearn's search on this matched primitive set fails to reliably recover Lorenz's y-dimension (the one dimension with a genuine cross term, x*z) at either degree tested, via two different failure modes (non-degree-respecting polynomial at degree=2; non-polynomial rational expression at degree=3). Treat symbolic-regression equation recovery on this system as "checked and failing at both preregistration-relevant degrees," not as a degree-3-specific artifact.

## 5. Pilot headline metric is secondary, not primary, per protocol (addressed; off-attractor-grid fix implemented 2026-08-16; amplitude-derived bounds fix implemented and reran 2026-08-16, RESOLVED)
PREREGISTRATION.md §8 designates "vector-field error off-trajectory on the confirmation trajectory" as the primary identifiability metric, with in-sample coefficient recovery as secondary. `experiments/pilot_chaos_vs_periodic.py` now generates an independent confirmation trajectory per run (different IC, same system/parameters/regime/noise, via `src/simulators.py`) and computes the normalized off-trajectory L2 vector-field error against the true analytic vector field, reported alongside coefficient recovery for every (system, regime, noise, seed) — see RESULTS.md "Pilot correction" section.
**Finding that motivated the fix:** the confirmation-trajectory metric disagreed with coefficient recovery for the logistic map's non-chaotic control — an independent-IC confirmation trajectory for a period-2 map converges onto the *same* 2-point cycle as training (basin-wide periodic attractor), so it never actually probed states outside the training support, and wrongly passed 5/5 despite 0/5 coefficient recovery.
**Fix (2026-08-16, see DECISION_LOG.md "Off-attractor evaluation grid"):** added a third metric, `vf_l2_err_off_attractor_grid`, evaluated on 500 points drawn from a genuinely wider-than-attractor domain (Lorenz: full pre-transient trajectory bounding box; logistic map: the map's actual analytic domain `[0,1]`, since the trajectory itself never contains the transient). Real run confirms the fix: logistic r=3.2 now reads 0/5 grid-VF-recovered at every noise level, correctly matching 0/5 coefficient recovery.
**Residual, honestly reported (not hidden because it complicates the fix):** for chaotic regimes at high degree/noise, VF-error metrics (both confirmation and the new grid) can still read "recovered" (<=10% error) in cells where exact coefficient recovery has already dropped (logistic degree=3/noise=5%: 2/5 coeff vs 5/5 both VF metrics; Lorenz degree=3/noise=1%: 3/5 coeff vs 5/5 both VF metrics). This is consistent with VF error being a more forgiving metric than exact-coefficient match, not a flaw in the grid construction — the grid's value-add is specific to periodic/fixed-point regimes (where it fully resolves the disagreement), not chaotic ones (where it tracks the confirmation metric almost exactly). **Resolved (2026-08-16, see DECISION_LOG.md "Off-attractor grid bounds derived from amplitude scale"):** replaced the hand-picked ±3.0 (harmonic) / ±2.0 (Duffing unforced) round-number bounds with `OFF_ATTRACTOR_GRID_SCALE * np.abs(states).max(axis=0)` (scale=3.0, per-dimension, derived from each run's own trajectory amplitude) in both `main_study.py` and `main_study_tier_b.py`; Lorenz/Rössler were already principled (trajectory bounding box) and untouched. Reran Tier A (132/132) and Tier B (210/210) in full under the new bounds after clearing checkpoints. Tier A result: the underlying `vf_l2_err_off_attractor_grid` values genuinely shifted (confirming the fix took effect) but zero pass/fail flips occurred in any harmonic/duffing_unforced cell — every previously-failing cell (already 0.5-10x past the 0.10 threshold) remained failing. Tier B comparison logged separately once its rerun completes. The methodological criticism (arbitrary, non-amplitude-derived bounds) is closed; no headline conclusion changed.

## 6. Multiplicity correction: RESOLVED (2026-08-16); Rössler blinding smoke test: RESOLVED (2026-08-16)
PREREGISTRATION.md §9 committed to "a Bonferroni-style correction across its fixed set of headline hypothesis tests" for the frozen main study. **Resolved:** the study never ran p-value hypothesis tests (all evidence is raw pass/fail counts against fixed thresholds), so a literal post-hoc p-value correction was not attempted — inventing one after seeing the data would itself be post-hoc metric selection. Instead, exact Clopper-Pearson binomial confidence intervals at a Bonferroni-adjusted level (k=6 fixed headline Tier B matched-pair comparisons, alpha_adj=0.00833, 99.17% CI) were computed directly from committed raw JSON. Every control-vs-chaotic interval pair remains non-overlapping under the adjustment (closest margin: symbolic-regression Lorenz, control CI upper 0.17 vs. chaotic CI lower 0.23) — the correction changes no headline conclusion but replaces the unquantified promise with an explicit, checkable number. Full table and method: DECISION_LOG.md "Multiplicity correction (PREREGISTRATION.md §9), resolved". Rössler/held-out-regime counts (n=5/cell) remain too small for a meaningful adjusted interval and are reported descriptively only, per §9's own small-n guidance.
**Resolved:** `scripts/run_rossler_blinding_smoke_test.py` now reproduces the shape-only mechanism as a committed, independently-runnable artifact (logs are gitignored/regenerable like every other tier's smoke test, per `.gitignore`'s `*.log`; run it directly to reproduce) — it calls `fit_rossler()` once on a small/fast condition and asserts only dict key names and Python types, never printing or reasoning about any numeric value. This closes the reproducibility gap going forward; it does not retroactively prove the original informal pre-launch check followed this exact procedure, only that the described mechanism is legitimate and reproducible.

## 7. "Chaos aids identifiability" may overstate what the matched-pair design tests (flagged by independent review, 2026-08-16; rich-non-chaotic-vs-chaos pair added 2026-08-17, gap partially closed)
The matched-pair contrasts carrying the headline claim (logistic period_2 vs. chaotic; Lorenz stable_fixed_point vs. classic_chaotic; and the Rössler confirmation's chaotic-vs-`c=3.0`-control) all pit a chaotic regime against a maximally *degenerate* non-chaotic control — a fixed point or short periodic orbit, close to rank-deficient for polynomial regression by construction (RESULTS.md's own logistic-map section already notes "rank <=2 on 2 unique support points"). The one genuinely rich, non-degenerate, non-chaotic regime in the whole study — the harmonic oscillator (a continuum of states on a closed orbit) — recovers coefficients at 5/5 in every noise cell at degree=2, just as cleanly as the chaotic regimes, and was never given a chaotic partner in Tier A/B's design (RESULTS.md's harmonic section already flags "no pair"). RESULTS.md line 19's own mechanistic account ("persistent-excitation... independent of noise or optimizer choice") already names the more defensible mechanism without the headline claim language fully carrying that qualifier.

**Partially closed (2026-08-17):** a harmonic/Rössler matched pair was added to Tier B (`experiments/main_study_tier_b.py`, 30 new conditions, RESULTS.md "Harmonic/Rössler matched pair"), directly testing chaos against this project's one rich non-chaotic control rather than a degenerate one. Result is genuinely mixed by method, not uniformly resolving the gap in either direction: **SINDy's primary (off-trajectory VF-error) metric shows chaos aiding identifiability even against the rich control** (Rössler 30/30 pass vs. harmonic 16/30, harmonic degrading at degree=3/higher noise) — this narrows the gap for SINDy specifically, the method carrying most of the original headline claim. **Symbolic regression's already-known chaos-hurts pattern replicates** (harmonic 30/30 joint-pass vs. rossler 10/30), unaffected either way by control degeneracy (driven by 3D cross-term dimensionality instead). **Koopman/EDMD's reversal does NOT replicate against the rich control** — rossler's one-step error is lower than or comparable to harmonic's in all 6 (noise,degree) cells, opposite the logistic/Lorenz pairs' 6-11x chaos-is-worse reversal — meaning the Koopman reversal itself was partly a degenerate-control artifact, not a universal chaos-vs-non-chaos property.

**Revised conclusion:** "chaos specifically confers an identifiability advantage" now has direct, non-degenerate-control support for SINDy (this project's primary/trusted comparator), but the Koopman/EDMD reversal — the study's most novel finding — is now shown to be pair-dependent rather than a general "chaos hurts Koopman" law. Only one rich-non-chaotic-vs-chaos pair has been tested (n=1 pair, not a systematic sweep); treat both the narrowing (SINDy) and the non-replication (Koopman) as evidence from a single matched pair, not a fully resolved question.
